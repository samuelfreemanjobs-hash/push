Systems Over Single Prompts

Chain Systems

Multi-step prompt sequences that compound intelligence. Copy each step — paste Step N's output into Step N+1. The chain is the product, not the individual prompt.

🔁
Self-Critique QC Chain
Draft → Critique → Polish · Adds a built-in quality review layer before final output
1
Draft
Act as an expert in [DOMAIN]. Your task: [OBJECTIVE]. Generate your most thorough initial response. Prioritize completeness over polish — you'll refine in a subsequent step. Do not hedge prematurely. Commit to a position.
2
Critique — Paste Step 1 output above this
[PASTE STEP 1 OUTPUT HERE] --- Now review the above with extreme rigor. Evaluate: 1. ACCURACY — Are all claims verifiable? What's unsupported? 2. COMPLETENESS — What's missing that a top expert would include? 3. CLARITY — What could confuse the target reader? 4. LOGIC — Any contradictions, gaps, or weak reasoning? 5. IMPACT — Does it actually achieve [OBJECTIVE]? Output a numbered critique. Be harsh. This is not the final output.
3
Polish — Paste Steps 1+2 above this
[PASTE ORIGINAL DRAFT HERE] [PASTE CRITIQUE HERE] --- Using the critique above as your guide, produce the definitive final version. Address every critique point explicitly. This is your final output — make it exceptional. Do not add a preamble explaining what you changed.

Theory of Mind Chain
Epistemic Map → Alien View → Audience Simulator · +29% accuracy on perspective-taking tasks (MIT study)
1
Epistemic Architect
Map the belief landscape around [TOPIC]. Identify 5 distinct perspectives: 1. What do domain experts believe, and why? 2. What do laypeople assume, often incorrectly? 3. What do critics/skeptics argue? 4. What hidden assumptions are baked into mainstream thinking? 5. What would a rigorous outsider find surprising or strange? Output: an epistemic map with confidence ratings for each claim.
2
Alien Collaborator
You are a researcher from a civilization that developed independently from Earth. You are studying [TOPIC] for the first time with no cultural assumptions or inherited biases. Examine it from first principles only. What patterns do you observe that humans seem to take for granted? What questions would you ask that humans never think to ask? What strikes you as deeply irrational or surprising about how humans approach this?
3
Audience Simulator — Paste Steps 1+2 above
[PASTE STEPS 1+2 OUTPUT HERE] --- Using the epistemic map and alien perspective above, create a tailored explanation of [TOPIC] for [TARGET AUDIENCE]. Preemptively address: - Their top 3 objections (stated as the audience would state them) - Their most likely confusion points - What they must believe FIRST before they'll accept the conclusion - One analogy from their world that makes this click instantly

Strategic Warfare Chain
Recon → Adversarial War Room → Decision Synthesis · For competitive analysis and high-stakes decisions
1
Strategic Recon
Conduct a full strategic reconnaissance of [COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE / CHALLENGE]. Map: 1. Key players and their current positions 2. Resource asymmetries (who has what advantage) 3. Hidden vulnerabilities in the current leaders 4. Tempo and timing — is there a window of opportunity? 5. Terrain advantages — what context favors which approach? Output: intel report with a threat/opportunity matrix.
2
Adversarial War Room
You are the strongest possible adversary of [MY POSITION / STRATEGY]. Your job: build the most devastating case AGAINST it. Find: - The fatal flaw I haven't noticed - The assumption I'm making that's most likely wrong - The attack vector that would collapse my strategy fastest - The scenario where my strengths become liabilities Don't hold back. I need to know the worst now, not after it's too late.
3
Decision Synthesis — Paste Steps 1+2 above
[PASTE STEPS 1+2 OUTPUT HERE] --- Holding the strategic map and adversarial critique simultaneously, identify the optimal path forward. Apply second-order thinking: what happens as a result of the result? Output: - Recommended strategy (specific, not general) - The single biggest bet embedded in it - 2 contingency branches if the core assumption fails - The decision I must make in the next 48 hours

Eisenhower Auditor
Priority Matrix → Ruthless Filter → Execution Plan · Cuts overwhelm, protects Q2 time
1
Priority Matrix
Here are all my current tasks, projects, and commitments: [YOUR COMPLETE LIST] For each item, classify without mercy: Q1 — Urgent + Important (crisis, deadline-driven) Q2 — Not Urgent + Important (strategy, growth, relationships) Q3 — Urgent + Not Important (other people's priorities, interruptions) Q4 — Not Urgent + Not Important (time wasters) Output as a labeled 2×2 matrix. Justify any Q1 classification — true Q1 is rare.
2
Ruthless Filter — Paste Step 1 above
[PASTE MATRIX HERE] --- Apply the 80/20 filter: - Q3 items: Who specifically should handle each instead of me? - Q4 items: Can it be eliminated entirely? What's the real cost of dropping it? - Q2 items: Which 20% will produce 80% of the meaningful results? Output: ELIMINATE: [specific items] DELEGATE: [item → who] FOCUS Q2: [top 3 items that will matter most in 6 months]
3
Execution Plan
[PASTE FILTERED LIST HERE] --- Build a weekly execution plan using only Q1 and top Q2 items. For each item, define: - DONE = [specific measurable outcome, not activity] - TIME BLOCK = [day + hours] - NEXT ACTION = [the single physical next step, verb + noun] Output as a structured weekly schedule. Flag any week with more than 2 Q1 items — that's a sign of chronic crisis mode, not priority.

The 9 Techniques
01
Zero-Shot
No examples. Direct instruction. Works when the model has strong pattern for the task. The foundation everything else builds on.
temp: 0.2–0.8 · varies by task
Classify this customer email as: complaint / inquiry / compliment. Email: [TEXT]
02
Few-Shot
2–5 input/output examples before the real task. Anchors format and style more reliably than any instruction. Most underused technique.
temp: 0.1–0.3
Input: "Product broke after 2 days" → Tone: Frustrated Input: "Love this!" → Tone: Positive Input: [TEXT] → Tone:
03
Chain of Thought
Force step-by-step reasoning. Add "think step by step" or show reasoning in examples. Reduces errors 40–70% on complex tasks. Always temp=0.
temp: 0.0 — deterministic only
Solve this step by step, showing your reasoning at each stage: [PROBLEM]
04
Self-Consistency
Generate multiple independent reasoning chains, take the majority answer. Dramatically reduces hallucination on factual questions.
temp: 0.5–0.8 · run 3–5 times
Think through this problem independently 3 times using different approaches. Then give the answer that appeared most consistently.
05
Tree of Thoughts
Branch multiple reasoning paths simultaneously, evaluate each, prune weak ones. Like a chess engine exploring moves. Best for open-ended problems.
temp: 0.6–0.8
Generate 3 distinct approaches to [PROBLEM]. Rate each 1–10. Develop only the highest-rated approach to completion.
06
ReAct
Interleave Reasoning and Acting — think, then use a tool, then think about the result. Foundation of all modern AI agents.
temp: 0.2–0.4
Thought: I need to find X Action: search("X") Observation: [result] Thought: Based on this, I should...
07
Step-Back
Ask a more abstract question first to establish principles, then apply to the specific problem. Grounds reasoning in fundamentals before specifics.
temp: 0.3
Step 1: What are the core principles governing [DOMAIN]? Step 2: Apply those principles to solve [SPECIFIC PROBLEM].
08
Self-Critique
Generate → Critique → Refine. The built-in QC loop. Model acts as its own adversarial reviewer between drafts. See the Chains tab for full implementation.
temp: 0.1–0.3
Draft: [GENERATE RESPONSE] Now critique your draft. What's weak? Final: Rewrite addressing every critique.
09
Auto Prompt Eng
Let the AI generate better prompts for itself. Provide goal + constraints, have it generate 5 prompt variants, evaluate each, use the winner.
temp: 0.5–0.7
Goal: [OBJECTIVE]. Generate 5 different prompts that could achieve this. Rate each 1–10 for clarity, specificity, and likely output quality. Recommend the best.

The Science of Tokens

Prompt engineering is empirical. These findings change how you structure every prompt you write. The data has clear implications — follow them.

The 60% Rule
Trim outputs to 60% of natural length for 97% accuracy. Longer ≠ better. Most AI responses carry 40% filler.

Reconsideration Red Flags
67.5% of token flips are deliberate reconsiderations. When you see these phrases, accuracy has already peaked — stop there.

⚠

⚠

⚠

⚠
