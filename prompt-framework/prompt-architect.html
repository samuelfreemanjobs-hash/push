Anthropic's core principles
Structure

Use XML tags as delimiters

Claude is trained to treat <tag> blocks as semantic sections. <context>, <task>, <examples>, <output_format> improve parsing accuracy significantly on long prompts.

Reasoning

Let Claude think before answering

Instruct "think step by step" or use adaptive thinking mode. For multi-step problems, reasoning before responding reduces errors by up to 40% vs. direct response.

Specificity

Replace every vague word

"Good output" tells Claude nothing. "A 300-word summary with 3 bullet points per section, no passive voice" is actionable. Measure quality in the prompt itself.

Examples

Show, don't just tell

2–3 input/output examples outperform pages of instructions. Include at least one "bad" example with an explanation — contrast is how Claude calibrates quality.

Persona

Expert roles improve output

Assigning a specific expert identity — not just "expert" but "senior ML engineer who has shipped 5 production recommendation systems" — activates domain-relevant reasoning patterns.

Framing

Positive constraints beat negative

"Avoid jargon" is weaker than "define every technical term the first time it appears." Telling Claude what to do instead is more effective than what not to do.

Agents

Break complex tasks into steps

For agentic work: give Claude a sequential plan it can execute. Number the steps, make dependencies explicit, and specify when to pause and verify before continuing.

Calibration

Tell Claude when to say "I don't know"

Explicitly permit uncertainty: "If you're unsure, say so and explain why rather than guessing." This reduces hallucination and builds trust in outputs you'll act on.
